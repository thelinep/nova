NOVA · how generation works

Every song and picture starts as a block of random numbers

NOVA's models don't paint or sing directly. They take a tensor full of noise and, step by step, predict how to remove it. The block shown here is that tensor. Press play or drag the slider to watch it go from static to structure.

This is a simulation with the same shapes and schedule NOVA uses. The Tensor view in NOVA shows the real thing while a job runs.

The basics

A tensor is a grid of numbers with a shape

The shape says how many numbers there are along each direction. NOVA writes shapes the way the models do, batch first: [1, 64, 750] means one item, 64 rows (channels), 750 columns (time). Colour here means value: amber is positive, blue is negative, dark is near zero.

Scalar []

0.42
One number. A seed, a CFG value, the noise level σ.

Vector [8]

A row of numbers. One token's embedding is a vector of 1,024 or 2,048 of these.

Matrix [4, 6]

Rows and columns. A song latent is a matrix: channels × time.

3-D [3, 4, 6]

Stacked matrices. An image latent is 4 stacked maps, each 1/8 of the picture's size.
The pipeline

What happens between your prompt and the file

These are the stages NOVA's Tensor view lights up, with the tensor shapes for a 30-second song and a 1024 × 1024 picture.

    One step up close

    Each step mixes less noise into the model's guess

    At every step the model looks at the noisy tensor and predicts the clean one, called x̂₀. The sampler then builds the next, less noisy tensor. ACE-Step 1.5 uses a straight-line (flow) mix, shown below on an 8 × 8 corner of a song latent. SDXL follows the same idea with a curved schedule.

    xt = (1 − σ) · x̂₀ + σ · noise    σ = 1.00

    Noise schedule and shift

    σ is how much noise is left. Shift keeps σ high for longer, so early steps settle the big picture (the song's structure) before details. NOVA runs ACE-Step 1.5 with shift 3.

    Speed and memory

    Why one engine is faster, and what fills 18 GB

    Guidance doubles the work

    With classifier-free guidance (CFG above 1) the model runs twice per step, once with your prompt and once without, and pushes the result away from the prompt-less guess:

    out = uncond + cfg · (cond − uncond)
    ACE-Step 1.5 turbo8 passes
    SDXL, 25 steps50 passes

    ACE-Step 1.5 turbo uses 8 steps at CFG 1: one pass per step. On your M3 Pro it made a 30-second song in 105–138 s, against 261–294 s for ACE-Step 1.

    The latent is tiny. The weights are not.

    The tensor you watch is small. Memory goes to the model weights, which stay loaded while the job runs.

    Song latent48,000
    Image latent65,536
    Song audio out2,880,000

    48,000 numbers is about 190 KB. The models behind it are several gigabytes each, which is why two engines loaded at once can push an 18 GB Mac to its limit. Watch the memory bar in the Tensor view.
